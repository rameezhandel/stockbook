Stockbook

Plain and functional, like you asked — the book where stock and prices live. No account, no signal, nothing leaves the phone.

Runners-up: Shopdesk · Counter · Partsbook

Built from your answers

Everything sells by the piece. One product, one stock count, one selling price — no pack or box variants to keep in step.

Customer name on every bill. Required — and it suggests names you've billed before, the ones who owe you money first.

Two ways to restock. Quick add when you just top up a bin; a purchase entry when a supplier delivers — that one updates the buying price.

Export and import. Settings writes one file with the whole shop in it — the only way onto a new phone, and the only backup you get when nothing syncs.

Left out on purpose

You skipped low-stock alerts, customer ledger, returns, receipts and barcodes, so none of them are here. Two I'd still argue for:

  • Voiding a bill. Without it, one mistyped bill puts stock permanently out of step with the shelf. I've left a quiet Void on the Bills screen — say the word and I'll pull it.
  • A low-stock line. With 300 hardware SKUs you won't notice the plugs are gone until someone asks.
Walk through it
  1. The phone opens on first-run setup — your name, then a few items, then stock and prices.
  2. Tap Sell, add an item, then tap the price and change it — for that bill only.
  3. Take a part payment and save. Check Items — stock has dropped.
  4. Open any item to edit the selling price, or add stock.
